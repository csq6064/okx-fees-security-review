## OKX trading fees explained

OKX does not use one universal fee for every customer and every product. Your rate depends on:

- Your fee tier
- Whether the order is a maker or taker order
- The trading product
- The trading pair

- Your recent trading volume or account assets

A **maker order** adds liquidity to the order book, usually through a limit order that does not fill immediately. A **taker order** removes liquidity by matching an order already available in the order book. Taker fees are often higher than maker fees.

### What the headline fee does not include

A low spot fee does not mean every transaction is cheap. Your total cost may also include:

- Spread when using instant conversion
- Network withdrawal fees
- Funding payments on perpetual contracts
- Borrowing interest for margin
- Slippage on less-liquid pairs
- Options premiums
- Liquidation-related costs
- Payment-provider fees when purchasing crypto with fiat

For example, OKX describes Convert as a simple way to exchange crypto, but active traders may prefer the order-book interface when they want more control over execution price. The convenience of a one-click conversion tool can come with a less transparent execution price than placing a limit order.

For futures and perpetuals, the trading fee is only one part of the calculation. Funding payments can move between long and short traders, and leverage increases both potential gains and potential losses. OKX’s risk documentation explains that leveraged positions can be automatically closed when margin falls below the required maintenance level.

## Which OKX products are useful?

### Spot trading

Spot trading is the most straightforward part of the platform. You buy or sell the asset itself, rather than opening a leveraged derivative position.

This is generally the better starting point if you:

- Are new to crypto exchanges
- Want to hold Bitcoin or another asset
- Do not need leverage
- Want to avoid liquidation mechanics
- Prefer a simple buy-and-sell workflow

Limit orders are useful when you want to specify the maximum price you will pay or the minimum price you will accept. Market orders are faster, but they can produce a different execution price when liquidity is thin or market conditions move quickly.

### Futures and perpetual swaps

OKX supports perpetual and expiry futures in eligible jurisdictions. These products allow traders to speculate on price movements without necessarily holding the underlying asset.

The tradeoff is risk. Leverage means a relatively small market movement can have a large impact on your margin. A position can be liquidated before the asset returns to your entry price, and funding payments can add to the cost of holding a perpetual position.

If you are evaluating OKX exchange for futures, check these details before opening a position:

1. The maximum leverage available for the specific contract.
2. Initial and maintenance margin requirements.
3. Funding-rate history and payment schedule.
4. Mark price and liquidation-price rules.
5. Whether cross or isolated margin is selected.
6. The exact fee rate for your account and contract.

OKX supports multiple account modes, including spot, futures, and advanced modes. The available products and risk-management rules differ between them.

### Trading bots

OKX includes automated trading tools for strategies such as grid trading, dollar-cost averaging, recurring purchases, and other predefined approaches.

Bots can be useful when you have a clear rule-based strategy and do not want to place every order manually. They do not remove market risk. A grid bot can continue placing trades while the market trends sharply in one direction, and a DCA strategy can keep buying while an asset falls.

Treat bot templates as execution tools, not as guaranteed-profit products. Review the range, order size, stop conditions, and funding source before activating any automated strategy.

### Copy trading

OKX offers spot and futures copy trading in eligible markets. Spot copy trading allows users to follow selected lead traders, while futures copy trading introduces the additional risks of leverage and liquidation.

OKX’s copy-trading documentation shows that users can review lead-trader statistics such as assets under management, profit and loss, return percentage, and lead-trader investment. The platform also states that lead traders may share part of their profits with copy traders under the applicable rules.

Performance data should be treated as historical information, not a promise. Before copying a trader, look beyond the headline return:

- How long has the account been active?
- What is the maximum drawdown?
- Does the strategy use leverage?
- Is the result driven by one unusually strong trade?
- How much capital is actually being used?
- What happens if the lead trader changes strategy?

For someone who only wants to buy and hold crypto, copy trading may add complexity without solving a real problem.

### Earn and lending products

OKX also lists products such as Simple Earn, on-chain earn, loans, and other yield-related services. These products can have different liquidity, counterparty, market, and smart-contract risks.

The yield percentage is not enough information by itself. Read whether the product is flexible or fixed-term, what assets are used, how redemption works, and whether the return depends on lending, staking, liquidity provision, or another mechanism.

## Is OKX safe?

No crypto exchange is risk-free, and proof-of-reserves data should not be treated as a complete substitute for personal custody. Still, OKX provides several security and transparency tools worth examining.

### Proof of reserves

OKX publishes proof-of-reserves reports and states that customer assets are backed on a 1:1 basis for the assets included in the report. Its current reserve page displays a September 2026 report covering primary assets and explains that the system uses Merkle trees and zk-STARK technology to verify account balances and on-chain holdings.

Proof of reserves can help users assess whether an exchange holds enough of the reported assets to cover customer account balances. It does not remove every risk. The report is a snapshot, only covers specified assets, and does not guarantee that every product, jurisdiction, or operational process is risk-free.

### Account security controls

You should activate the security features available to your account, including:

- Authenticator-based two-factor authentication
- Withdrawal address whitelisting
- Anti-phishing codes
- Strong, unique password
- Device and login monitoring
- Withdrawal confirmation controls
- API restrictions if you use automated trading

Never approve a withdrawal you did not initiate. A platform’s security architecture cannot protect funds if an attacker controls your email, phone, device, or authentication method.

### Security fund and leveraged products

OKX describes a segregated Security Fund intended to help manage certain counterparty losses associated with leveraged trading and negative-equity events. The fund is owned and controlled by OKX, and users do not have direct ownership rights over its assets.

That distinction matters. The existence of a security or insurance fund does not mean every trading loss is reimbursed. It is part of the exchange’s risk-management structure, not personal insurance for ordinary market losses.
